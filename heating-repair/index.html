Warning Signs for Heating Repair

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • Cold air, or no air at all, when the thermostat calls for heat
  • The system starting and stopping every few minutes without warming the house
  • Banging at startup caused by burners lighting late
  • Clicking and failed ignition attempts before the furnace gives up
  • Energy costs climbing even though the house feels colder
  • Uneven heat, with certain rooms that never warm up
  • A heat pump's outdoor unit encased in frost or ice
  • Strange smells from the vents, especially rotten egg or burning

Why Professional Heating Repair Matters

Combustion is what makes heating repair different from the rest of home comfort work. A late light-off booms unburned gas inside the heat exchanger and stresses the metal each time it happens. A cracked exchanger can leak combustion gases into the air your family breathes, and a flue that stops drafting pushes carbon monoxide into the living space instead of outdoors. None of these problems make themselves obvious to someone without training and instruments. Diagnosis has its own traps as well. Short cycling can point to a failing limit switch or to a furnace protecting itself from a clogged filter, and a flame sensor cleaned without finding out why it fouled simply sets up the next lockout.

Instruments and judgment are what our experienced technicians bring to both sides of that problem. We measure the flame signal instead of assuming it, inspect the heat exchanger, verify draft at the flue, and check temperature rise against the data plate so blower speed and burner output are matched. With heat pumps, the defrost board, sensors, and reversing valve get tested before anyone condemns a compressor, and we look at whether the backup strips have been running up your bill for weeks. Every repair we finish is proven safe and correct over a full heating cycle, which is the standard you should expect from anyone working on the equipment that keeps your family warm.

Heating Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly and put one on every level of the home
  • Avoid crowding the furnace with storage, which can starve the burners of air
  • Take booming ignition or new rattles seriously; both are cheap to fix early and expensive late
  • Move solvents, paints, and pool or laundry chemicals away from the furnace to protect the burners from corrosive vapor
  • Change the filter monthly in heating season; starved airflow trips limit switches and cracks exchangers early
  • Have the system serviced each autumn, before the weather tests every weak component
  • Clear snow, leaves, and overgrown shrubs from around the heat pump so defrost works properly
